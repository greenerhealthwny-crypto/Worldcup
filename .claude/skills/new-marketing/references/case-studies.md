# The New Marketing: case studies and lessons

Short examples from the book, each with the principle it illustrates. Use them to make recommendations concrete. The figures are as reported in the book (2020); don't present them as current data.

## Customer insight and listening
- **Shelter Pet Project × Nielsen Consumer Neuroscience.** EEG and eye tracking showed viewers engaged when the dog "Jules" was on screen but that he pulled attention from the text, branding and call to action. Separating the text onto its own Jules-free screen was followed by a 133% rise in website visits and a 28% rise in adoption-portal searches. *Lesson:* measure nonconscious attention and emotion moment by moment, and optimize specific scenes.
- **Lego (early 2000s).** Chasing movie tie-ins and electronic gimmicks led to a ~$300M loss and near bankruptcy. The company recovered by really listening to fans and parents, and through a "small data" visit with an 11-year-old whose prized possession was a worn pair of skate shoes, which showed that kids value mastery. Lego returned to its core bricks and became hugely profitable. *Lesson:* stay true to what customers love, use small data for causation, and admit weaknesses.
- **Southwest Airlines winter video.** Social listening surfaced chatter about how airlines handle winter weather, and the team quickly filmed "The Bucket Man" de-icing planes in Denver. *Lesson:* listen, then respond fast with content that reinforces a core value (safety).
- **Kia Super Bowl ad.** Kellogg's ad review showed how audience responses reveal whether creative lands. *Lesson:* test creative with real audiences.
- **sparks & honey.** A daily "culture briefing" scans thousands of signals to turn culture into real-time brand opportunities. *Lesson:* be culture-centric, not just customer-centric.

## Brand, trust and purpose
- **Warby Parker.** "Buy a Pair, Give a Pair," a convenient try-at-home model, and a clear stance on quality, style and social good. *Lesson:* authentic purpose plus great CX makes an unforgettable brand.
- **Southwest.** Its vision ("most loved, most efficient, most profitable airline") filters every employee decision, and it leads in customer satisfaction. *Lesson:* the brand is the filter for business decisions.
- **Nordstrom tire legend.** A probably apocryphal story of a refund for snow tires the store never sold persists because customers believe it. *Lesson:* consistent service over time creates trust and legend.
- **Patagonia.** Sells co-branded apparel only to B Corps. *Lesson:* legacy brands can live their purpose, even at a cost.
- **Glossier.** "Democratize beauty" through real customers' #GetReadyWithMe videos. *Lesson:* purpose expressed through community content.
- **United Airlines (Calkins).** People fly for schedule and price. *Lesson:* purpose isn't the purchase driver in every category.
- **Aetna.** Repositioned a 160-year-old brand around holistic health ("You don't join us, we join you"). *Lesson:* interrogate heritage to shape the future.
- **Audi A4, "Intelligence is the new rock and roll."** Positioned as the progressive luxury brand; the in-car tech followed from a long BVP of challenging industry standards. *Lesson:* positioning grows out of a consistent BVP.

## Design and experience
- **Apple mouse (David Kelley Design / IDEO).** Borrowed an Atari trackball, flipped it and shrank it into an affordable mouse. *Lesson:* first-principles, human-centered design.
- **Skype redesign rollback.** An overloaded redesign failed, and the fix was removing features. *Lesson:* design around the core job to be done; sometimes less is WOW.
- **Spaghetti tower challenge.** Kindergartners beat MBAs and CEOs by prototyping without status games. *Lesson:* iterate fast, check your ego.
- **Juicero.** A $699 Wi-Fi juicer whose packets could be squeezed by hand; it collapsed. *Lesson:* needless complexity kills.
- **PillPack.** Pre-sorted, dated medication packets that remove a daily hassle; acquired by Amazon for nearly $1B. *Lesson:* simplicity that solves a real problem.
- **Netflix, ALDI, Google.** Ranked the world's simplest brands. *Lesson:* if you can't explain what you do in a sentence, simplify.
- **IKEA field trip (Rutgers MBS).** Students study human-centered retail design firsthand. *Lesson:* learn design thinking by observing real experiences.
- **Bob buys a smartphone (Customer Choreography Map).** The current state is confusing choices, unhelpful staff and billing issues, with no loyalty. The ideal state is helpful online info and demos, low-pressure expert advice, onboarding and training, a loyalty program, and Bob posting positive reviews. *Lesson:* close the gaps between current and ideal journeys.

## Content and storytelling
- **Coca-Cola ("Coca-Cola stories, not stories by Coca-Cola").** If a story works without the brand, it isn't the brand's story.
- **Budweiser Clydesdales.** A symbol that carries the heritage story for decades.
- **Dollar Shave Club.** A deadpan launch video (25M+ views) plus MEL Magazine and in-box humor built a $1B brand. *Lesson:* entertaining, useful content beats sales pitches.
- **Blendtec "Will It Blend?"** Blending iPhones and golf balls drew 100M+ views and a reported 1,000% sales increase, while a conventional product video drew a few thousand. *Lesson:* entertainment plus product proof.
- **American Express (Travel + Leisure, Food & Wine).** Early branded publishing aimed at affluent customers.
- **NewsCred Top 50 (Land Rover Stories, Nike Breaking2, Visit Seattle, Fidelity, IKEA Ideas, Glossier).** Strong visuals, built-in challenges, useful tips, people-first voice, and alignment with the brand story.
- **Subaru Impreza.** Life-stage vignettes ("Moving Out") sell longevity and family security through emotion.
- **L'Oréal "Because I'm worth it" and Apple "Get a Mac."** Enduring ad campaigns show paid storytelling still works when the idea is strong.

## Distribution and integration
- **Microsoft Xbox Adaptive Controller (Super Bowl 2019).** The story of Owen, a boy with special needs, plus "When everybody plays, we all win" tied a brand truth to an inclusion movement. It debuted on TV, then spread via YouTube and social, and thousands of employees emailed pride. *Lesson:* a brand truth plus cultural relevance, amplified across channels and by employees.
- **Alpha (Nis Frome).** Early diverse bets on a podcast and a Medium publication paid off. *Lesson:* follow your audience's leaders and experiment early.
- **Stranger Things product placement.** Nostalgic, story-native placements (Coke, Eggo) were valued at ~$15M. *Lesson:* placement works when it serves the story.
- **Starbucks.** Stands for the customer experience (the "third place"). *Lesson:* stand for something.
- **REI #OptOutside.** Closed on Black Friday and invited everyone outdoors, which built a community around the hashtag. *Lesson:* purpose-driven actions create earned media.

## Employees and influencers
- **Domo social employee program.** Moved onboarding into an LMS, tied completion to a $500 tech benefit, ran a few contests a year around big events (engineers won when contests ran), and used one global advocacy platform with regional admins. *Lesson:* make advocacy easy, rewarded and scalable.
- **Microsoft employees on the Owen ad.** Emotional internal reactions show employees are their own media channel.
- **Ice Bucket Challenge.** 17M+ videos and $220M raised for ALS. *Lesson:* people-driven, viral participation amplifies causes and brands.
- **Influential (IBM Watson matching).** Pairs brands with influencers by demographics, topic relevance and psychographics, and measures store visits, test drives and ticket sales rather than impressions. *Lesson:* micro-influencers often out-engage celebrities; disclose sponsorships.
- **IBM "Uniquely Generation Z" study.** Gen Z values quality and value, authenticity, and control over personal data; still shops in stores; and influences family purchases. *Lesson:* deliver fundamentals and transparency.

## Personal branding
- **Dorie Clark.** Laid off in 2001, she built a strategy consultancy by standing out on reputation rather than price, and became an author, professor and speaker. *Lesson:* a strong personal brand is career insurance.
- **Tom Peters, "The Brand Called You" (1997).** Apply brand-building logic to your career; differentiate, get your name out, deliver value.

## Data and AI
- **Netflix.** Viewing data drives recommendations and billions in content decisions, a "twenty-first-century analytical model." *Lesson:* data in, branding out.
- **UBank "Mia" (IBM Watson).** An AI agent answering routine home-loan questions frees staff for complex issues. *Lesson:* chatbots excel at structured, repetitive requests.
- **Everlane and Facebook M chatbots.** Scaled back when requests were too varied. *Lesson:* match bot scope to the use case.
- **Nike Fit and Disney MagicBand.** Computer vision for shoe fit; wristbands that remove friction in parks. *Lesson:* use data and AI to remove friction from the experience.
- **Alexa and voice commerce (Exxon/Mobil pay-at-pump).** Voice makes unbranded ordering easy. *Lesson:* build brand salience so people ask for you by name.
- **Cambridge Analytica and Facebook's $5B FTC fine.** Data misuse destroys trust and brings regulation (GDPR, CCPA). *Lesson:* transparency and control are part of the brand.
- **Dell data protection.** Security, transparent documentation and process design go beyond GDPR to build trust.
- **Phrasee.** AI copy generation with a deliberately diverse team to reduce bias. *Lesson:* human checks on AI.
