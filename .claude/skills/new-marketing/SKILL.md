# The New Marketing

A working playbook distilled from *The New Marketing: Think Like a Brand in the Digital Age* by Cheryl Burgess and Mark Burgess (SAGE, 2020). The book's thesis: marketing has moved from interrupting people with ads to **earning trust by delivering value, experiences and stories, integrated across every touchpoint, powered by data and AI but led by humans**. It is "a little bit art, a little bit science."

Use this skill to guide strategy, produce deliverables (personas, positioning statements, content plans, journey maps, advocacy programs, personal brand plans), and critique existing marketing against the book's standards.

## Core principles

Treat these as the non-negotiables. When reviewing any marketing work, check it against each one.

1. **Customer-centric, not product-centric.** Start from who the customer is, what they struggle with, and what they want. Walk in their shoes; demographics alone don't tell you who they are.
2. **Start with why.** People buy the reason behind what you do, not just the product (Sinek's Golden Circle: why → how → what). Every brand, and every person, needs a clear purpose and a brand value proposition (BVP) that answers *what value do we deliver, what need do we satisfy, why do customers buy from us, why should they care*.
3. **Trust is the currency.** A brand is a promise and a signal of what the customer will get. Trust is slow to build, fast to lose, and built by consistently delivering on the promise. Trust in ads, institutions and CEOs is low; trust in peers, employees and word of mouth is high.
4. **Purpose must be authentic and lived.** Purpose is a real reason for existing that creates value for stakeholders, not a retrofitted slogan or cause-washing. Customers spot fakes. Purpose matters a lot in some categories and little in others (a flight's time and price may still decide the sale), so don't force it.
5. **Clarity and simplicity win.** Customers must see your value quickly and effortlessly. A menu of a hundred cuisines hides the one great dish. Simplicity is a competitive differentiator; needless complexity (Juicero) kills products.
6. **Emotion beats information.** Marketing has moved from the attention economy to the emotion economy. Stories are far more memorable and persuasive than facts alone. People forget what you said, but they remember how you made them feel.
7. **Content is value, not a pitch.** Content marketing is a dialogue that helps, informs or entertains, and it must tie back to the brand story. Use the 80/20 rule: about 80% valuable content, at most 20% self-promotion.
8. **Experience is the brand.** A brand is defined by the people who experience it. Design for WOW across every touchpoint. Customers compare you with the best digital experience they've had anywhere, not only with your competitors.
9. **Integrate everything.** Customers don't care which department owns a channel. Deliver the **right message to the right person, in the right place, at the right time**, consistently across paid, owned and earned media. Integration is a process, not a project.
10. **Build from the inside out.** Culture eats strategy for breakfast. Employees who understand and believe in the mission are the most credible marketing channel ("the social employee"). Happy employees make happy customers.
11. **Data in, branding out, with a human touch.** Use data and AI to answer specific business questions, not for their own sake. Combine big data (patterns) with small data (close human observation). AI augments people; it doesn't replace judgment, empathy or strategy. Guard against bias and too much automation.
12. **Earn the right to use data.** Privacy and transparency are part of the brand. Tell people what you collect, why, and how it's used; give them control and a fair value exchange.
13. **Measure, learn, revise.** Set specific, quantifiable goals and KPIs. Revisit personas at least quarterly, test positioning with real buyers, and iterate. "The first designs are rarely best, and the best designs are rarely first."
14. **Think Big M, not just small m.** Strategic marketing (where to play, how to win, how to frame value, how it ties to P&L and demand generation) matters more than tactical optimization of clicks and likes.
15. **Stay future-ready.** Adapt to shifts like mobile-first behavior, voice, video, AR/VR, Gen Z and Gen Alpha, but keep the fundamentals (4 Ps, STP, 3 Cs) as lenses.

## How to use this skill

1. **Identify the job** from the request, then open the matching framework in [references/frameworks.md](references/frameworks.md):

   | User wants… | Framework(s) |
   |---|---|
   | Understand the customer / audience research | §1 Consumer insight methods, §2 Buyer personas (7 steps), pain-point analysis |
   | Define or sharpen a brand | §3 SWOT, BVP, positioning statement, brand purpose (Credle's past/present/future) |
   | Map or fix the customer journey / CX | §4 Journey models, §5 Customer Choreography Map, design thinking and IDEO rules |
   | Content marketing plan | §6 Seven-Step Content Strategy Blueprint, content template (5 Ws + How), storytelling, video |
   | Choose channels / media plan | §7 Trifecta of media, POEM, rolling thunder, mobile-first, O2O/omnichannel, ABM |
   | Full integrated marketing strategy | §8 Brand Choreography 7-step roadmap and the social ecosystem |
   | Employee advocacy / influencers / Gen Z | §9 Social employee pilot plan, social media policy, influencer tiers, Gen Z |
   | Personal brand (resume, LinkedIn, career) | §10 Personal branding 7 steps and workbook questions |
   | Data, AI, martech, metrics, privacy | §11 Data and AI principles, KPI selection, BQ matrix, privacy checklist |
   | CMO / org / future strategy | §12 Big M vs small m, CMO priorities, future trends |

2. **Ask only for context you can't infer.** Useful inputs: the brand or person, category, B2B or B2C, target buyers, goals and KPIs, current channels, constraints (budget, team). If the user wants a quick draft, make reasonable assumptions and label them.

3. **Produce concrete deliverables** using the templates in the reference file: filled-in persona cards, positioning statements in the book's formula, SWOT grids, current vs ideal journey tables, content calendars, POEM mixes, pilot plans, KPI tables.

4. **Critique against the core principles.** When reviewing existing work, go through the principles above and flag gaps. Examples: no clear why or BVP; product-centric copy; inconsistent messaging across channels; too much self-promotion; a single catch-all persona; vanity metrics without business outcomes; data use without transparency.

5. **Ground advice in examples** from [references/case-studies.md](references/case-studies.md) when it helps make a point concrete (e.g., Lego's return to its core, Warby Parker's purpose, Dollar Shave Club's launch video, Southwest's social listening).

## Quick formulas

- **Brand positioning statement (company):** To [target market], [brand] is the brand of [competitive framework] that provides [meaningful point of difference].
- **Personal positioning statement:** For [target market], [brand/you] provides [point of difference] because [reason to believe].
- **BVP questions:** What value do we deliver? What customer needs do we satisfy? Why do customers buy from us? Why should they care? Focus on the problem you solve, not what you sell.
- **Content strategy 5 Ws + How:** Who (buyer persona), What (content types), When (best time and frequency), Where (channels), Why (purpose / brand story / cause), How (delivery and metrics/ROI).
- **Sticky messages (Heath brothers' SUCCESs):** Simple, Unexpected, Concrete, Credible, Emotional, Stories.
- **Brand Choreography goal:** right message → right person → right place → right time, across every touchpoint.
- **Social posting rule:** 80% valuable content, 20% promotional.
- **Employee learning model:** 70% on the job / 20% from peers and managers / 10% formal training.

## Tone and style for outputs

- Be practical and specific. Name the persona, the channel, the metric, and the next step.
- Lead with the customer and the why. Show how each tactic connects to the brand story and business goals.
- Favor authentic, human, helpful messaging over hype. Flag anything that reads as salesy, inauthentic, or intrusive.
- When recommending AI or data tools, pair them with the human judgment, ethics and privacy considerations that go with them.
