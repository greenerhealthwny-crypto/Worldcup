# Nursery Biosecurity (keeping pests out)

Distilled from *Biosecurity Manual for the Nursery Production Industry: Reducing the risk of pests entering and becoming established in your production nursery*, Version 1.0, Plant Health Australia (PHA) with Nursery and Garden Industry Australia (NGIA), 2010. "Manual p. N" refers to that edition.

**How this differs from `plant-containment-greenhouse`:** that skill keeps research organisms *in* (containment for GE plants, pathogens, quarantine). This one keeps outside pests *out* of a commercial growing operation (exclusion and hygiene). If a request involves both (e.g., a research greenhouse that also wants clean stock), use both skills.

In the manual, a **pest** is any insect, mite, snail, nematode, pathogen (disease) or weed that may harm plants or plant products. **Exotic** pests are not present in Australia; **established** pests are.

## Currency warning
The manual is a 2010 Australian document. Before giving regulatory or contact details:
- The **Exotic Plant Pest Hotline 1800 084 881** is the number the manual gives (Australia). Tell the user to confirm it is still current on the Plant Health Australia or their state agriculture department website.
- State department names and phone numbers in the manual (Manual p. 22) are outdated, because departments have since been renamed and restructured. Don't quote them; tell the user to look up their current state/territory biosecurity agency.
- Pest status changes. A pest the manual calls exotic may have since arrived or spread (check current status before calling it "exotic to Australia"). Program names (NIASA, BioSecure HACCP, EcoHort, Nursery Production FMS) may have been rebranded or merged; describe them as the manual does and suggest checking with the national nursery industry body (NGIA in the manual; it may have been renamed since, so confirm the current name).
- Outside Australia: keep the practices, swap in the user's national plant protection organization and reporting line.

## How to run it
1. **Establish the operation:** type (production nursery, greenhouse, propagation, growing media supplier, retail with production), location/region, crops (especially Myrtaceae, citrus, grapes, oaks/rhododendrons, greenhouse ornamentals), who comes on site (staff, public, contractors, deliveries), and what they want (plan, SOP, signage, checklist, records, pest ID help, incident steps). Ask once for missing essentials; otherwise state assumptions.
2. **Read the matching reference file(s).**
3. **Map entry pathways** for this operation: people (boots, clothes, hands), vehicles and machinery, inputs (media, containers, fertiliser, propagation material, motherstock), water, waste, neighbouring/abandoned properties and native or amenity plantings, overseas travel. Rank them for this site.
4. **Produce the deliverable** using the formats below.
5. **Check against the quality rules**, fix misses, then reply. End with at most one line suggesting a next step (e.g., turn it into signage text, a staff induction, or record sheets).

## Output formats
- **On-farm biosecurity plan:** site summary → key threats (exotic and established) → entry pathways ranked → controls per pathway (people, vehicles/equipment, inputs, water, waste) → surveillance schedule and records → suspect-pest response → responsibilities and induction → review date.
- **SOP / checklist:** purpose → who → steps (short, checkable) → records kept → what to do if something's wrong.
- **Suspect pest response card:** the three immediate actions → call the hotline (confirm number) → don't send samples without instructions → record what you saw (photos, location, date, plant, symptoms) → restrict operations until ID.
- **Pest ID help:** match described symptoms to the fact sheets, give look-alikes, and always say "report it; don't self-diagnose an exotic pest". Never confirm an exotic detection; only experts can.
- **Signage text:** short, firm, tells visitors to report to the office/manager and register before entering production areas, with office phone, mobile and/or UHF channel.

## Quality rules
- Be specific to the operation and its crops; no generic biosecurity filler.
- Every control ties to a named pathway.
- Always include records (the manual stresses traceability: trace-back of inputs, trace-forward of dispatched plants, and surveillance/pest-management records).
- Treat early detection and immediate reporting as non-negotiable; the manual links it to eradication success and to market access ("evidence of absence").
- Don't invent chemical rates. The manual names example wash-down products (Septone Truckwash®, Castrol Farmcleanse®, Virkon®) and points to BioSecure HACCP Appendix 1 for disinfectant use; tell users to follow product labels and local regulations.
- Flag outdated contacts/program names per the currency warning.
