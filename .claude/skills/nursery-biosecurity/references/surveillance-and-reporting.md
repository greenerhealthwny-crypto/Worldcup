# Pest Surveillance and Reporting

Source: Manual pp. 10–11 (2010). Paraphrased.

## What surveillance is
Looking for and recording the presence, absence and population levels of pests across the nursery. Regular monitoring is core nursery management and the best chance of spotting a new pest soon after it arrives. It is a key part of BioSecure HACCP.

## Why it's needed
- **Exotic pest eradication:** early detection improves the odds of eradication or containment within a region. If eradication isn't feasible, early detection plus government/industry preparedness (emergency chemical registrations, permits to import biocontrol agents, awareness material, diagnostic training) speeds an effective response.
- **Market access:** export and interstate markets may require "evidence of absence" for exotic and some established pests. Industry and government must show through surveillance that pests were looked for and not found.
- **Better pest management:** regular inspection of established pest levels improves decisions and keeps stock healthy for clients.
- **Pest status:** nursery-level surveillance feeds regional efforts and the national presence/absence status.

## Recording
- Record all surveillance (exotic and established pests). Records support outbreak response and industry surveillance.
- Add priority exotic pests to your existing monitoring datasheets; it's an efficient way to record them.
- Monitoring guidelines are in the BioSecure HACCP manual; see record-templates.md for a crop monitoring layout.
- Know what your plants normally look like; keep written and photographic records of unusual observations.

## If you find a suspected exotic pest
**Immediately:**
1. Mark the location and limit access for people and equipment.
2. Wash hands, clothes and boots that touched affected plants or growing media.
3. Restrict operations in the area until the pest is identified.

**Report** right away to the Exotic Plant Pest Hotline (manual: 1800 084 881; confirm current). Calls go to an experienced person in your state/territory government, who asks what you saw and may arrange sample collection.
- **Don't send samples** until you've spoken with the state department; they advise on sample type, packaging, handling and transport to the assigned diagnostic lab.
- In some states the hotline runs business hours only; after hours leave full contact details and a brief description for follow-up.
- Every report is taken seriously, checked and treated confidentially.

## The Emergency Plant Pest Response Deed (EPPRD) (as of 2010)
- Formal, legally binding agreement between PHA, the Australian and state/territory governments and plant industry signatories.
- NGIA was a signatory: it sits at the decision table and contributes funding when an approved Response Plan eradicates an Emergency Plant Pest.
- NGIA and its members are responsible for reporting suspect pests. Earlier detection means an eradication response is more likely to be mounted and to succeed.
- Approved Response Plans include **Owner Reimbursement Costs** for growers' direct costs from eradication. Point users to PHA for current EPPRD and reimbursement details.
