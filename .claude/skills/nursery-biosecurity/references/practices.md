# Biosecurity Practices

Source: Manual pp. 2–21 (2010). Paraphrased.

## Six easy ways to protect your production nursery (Manual pp. 2–3)
1. **Be aware of biosecurity threats.** Know the most important exotic threats; run a biosecurity induction covering hygiene for people, equipment and vehicles.
2. **Use only clean, pest-free, preferably certified inputs.** Growing media, fertiliser, propagation material from reputable suppliers, ideally NIASA-accredited or BioSecure HACCP-certified. Keep records of all inputs.
3. **Keep it clean.** Workers, visitors, vehicles, raw materials and equipment are decontaminated or come from a clean source before entering and leaving. Have a designated visitor area and vehicle/personnel disinfecting facilities.
4. **Check your production nursery.** Monitor crops and raw materials often; know what normal looks like; keep written and photo records of anything unusual.
5. **Abide by the law.** Know the laws protecting the industry, agriculture and your region.
6. **Report anything unusual** immediately (Exotic Plant Pest Hotline; confirm the current number).

These practices should be part of everyday management. They also support the NGIA on-farm programs (NIASA, EcoHort, BioSecure HACCP).

## Why it matters (Manual pp. 4–5)
- Biosecurity = measures at national, regional or property level to stop new pests arriving and spreading and to deal with them if they arrive. On a property it is the responsibility of everyone working or visiting.
- An established new pest costs you through monitoring, cultural practices, extra chemicals and labour; lower yield/quality; and lost markets.
- Australia's isolation means relatively few overseas pests are present. Freedom from them underpins profitability, trade, and new market negotiations.

## Regional biosecurity (Manual p. 5)
- Threats come from neighbouring nurseries or farms (operating or abandoned), other commercial plantings, native vegetation, amenity plantings, and plant movement within the region.
- Actions: hold or join regional biosecurity meetings; raise awareness of key threats; map production locations; agree protocols for moving produce within the region; engage the local council.
- Every agricultural business in a region is hit by an incursion (lost production, market restrictions, compliance costs, quarantined produce, crop destruction).
- Property biosecurity underpins regional, which underpins national. Needs community engagement, knowledge of the region and its threats, available expertise and resources, and everyone doing surveillance and reporting.

## Industry programs (Manual pp. 6–7, as of 2010)
- **Nursery Production Farm Management System (FMS):** umbrella for NIASA (Nursery Industry Accreditation Scheme Australia), EcoHort and BioSecure HACCP best-practice programs. Open to production nurseries and growing media suppliers.
- **Traceability** in the FMS: trace-back of plant material and inputs to source; trace-forward of plants that left the property; records of surveillance and pest management.
- **BioSecure HACCP:** the on-farm biosecurity program for nurseries and growing media businesses; validates many NIASA practices; gives decision tools to identify and mitigate risks; encourages the strictest possible internal quarantine and recording actions at critical control points; supports domestic and international market access.
- Confirm current program names and owners before advising.

## Product management (Manual pp. 12–13)

**Growing media, propagating material, other inputs**
- Pests easily arrive with inputs (media, containers, fertiliser) and plant material.
- You can't visually judge propagation material's health: viruses, viroids, phytoplasmas and other pests may be symptomless. Use "clean" (tested, no detections) and, where possible, certified material.
- Buy only from trusted sources (NIASA-accredited or BioSecure HACCP-certified) that can give records of source, treatment and testing history.
- Inspect propagation material and inputs thoroughly.
- Keep a register of all inputs entering the property: source with contact details, planting or storage location, quantity, date of use.
- Ask suppliers for source information and testing timetable.
- Maintain and monitor motherstock plants to the same standard as production areas.
- Follow BioSecure HACCP procedures.

**Potting and propagating facilities**
- Separate them from production areas.
- Wash and disinfect benches and tools regularly, preferably between each batch of media or plant material.
- Clean and disinfect pots before use.
- Collect and discard spilt material and plant waste regularly.

**Water management**
- Contaminated water spreads pests through production areas.
- Test water storages regularly and monitor surrounding vegetation for pests.
- Keep storage surrounds free of plant waste and other infestation sources.
- Don't let production run-off enter irrigation sources without catchment and treatment first.

**Production nursery waste**
- Good hygiene reduces cross-contamination and pest breeding sites; pair with an effective pest monitoring/management program.
- A "spray diary" record should go with each consignment of plant material. (NGIA had a pesticide application best-practice CD with a pesticide management diary.)
- Collect all plant waste and spilled media and remove it from production areas; dispose away from production areas and water sources.
- Suitable disposal: hot composting or a dedicated waste management facility.

## People (Manual pp. 14–15)

**Biosecurity signs**
- Tell visitors biosecurity matters and they share responsibility; show your commitment to hygiene, safety and auditable systems.
- Place at the main gate, external entrances, visitor parking and wash-down facilities.
- Signs at entrances or near storages direct visitors to contact the owner/manager and register before entering production areas, with office phone, mobile and/or UHF channel.
- (PHA could supply signs.)

**Managing people movement**
- Biggest risk: pests carried in growing media and plant material on vehicles, equipment, boots and clothing, especially from other nurseries and horticultural regions.
- Keep a visitor register (movements and safety).
- Brief all workers, contractors and visitors on your measures.
- Footwear and clothing free of soil and plant material on entry and exit.
- Provide scrubbing brushes, footbaths, boot covers, rubber boots and protective clothing (e.g., disposable overalls) for entering/leaving or moving from contaminated to clean areas.
- Install sealed or compacted, well-drained pathways through propagation and production areas.
- Display simple biosecurity messages where staff congregate (staff rooms, sheds).

**Contractors and utility providers** (utilities, earthmovers, transport, researchers)
- High risk because they move between properties.
- Gate signs raise awareness, especially with power, water, gas and communications providers.
- Require vehicles and equipment to be cleaned before work; provide a wash-down bay.
- Tell contractors about any declared or notifiable pests already on your property so you don't become the source for others.

**Overseas travellers**
- Recently returned travellers, especially those who visited nurseries, farms or produce markets, are a threat; pests have been intercepted at the border and brought in unknowingly before. Air travel puts exotic pests hours away.
- Make sure they've cleaned boots and clothes before entering.

## Equipment and vehicles (Manual pp. 17–19)

**Movement of vehicles and machinery**
- Vehicles, sprayers, tractors and hand tools carry pests in adhering media and plant material.
- You can't stop all movement, but: use dedicated nursery vehicles, wash down on concrete pads, and refuse dirty machinery.
- Ask contractors, re-sellers, service providers, delivery drivers (media, compost, mulch, fertiliser) and earthmovers to clean before entering production or storage areas, or site storage so it can be reached without crossing production areas.
- Businesses open to the public or to growers (field days, demos) carry higher risk: provide parking away from production.
- Inspect every vehicle entering for pests and plant material; record in a register.
- Hose off and disinfect machinery in the wash-down area before moving between properties.
- Clean vehicle floors and tyres, especially after visiting other properties.
- Carry visitors in your own vehicle where possible.
- Minimise vehicle movement in production areas, especially on wet soil; stick to regular paths.
- Clean borrowed and second-hand equipment before it comes in.
- Clean tools and equipment regularly, preferably with a disinfectant or bleach solution (BioSecure HACCP Appendix 1).
- Inspecting and cleaning is cheaper than managing a new pest.

**Wash-down facilities.** Should:
- Be easily accessible, between the driveway and property roads.
- Be isolated from production areas.
- Have power and high-pressure water.
- Have a sealed (concrete or bitumen) or packed-gravel surface.
- Not drain into a waterway or production area.
- Have a sump or collection area for inspection and waste management.

How to clean:
- Clean from the top down so cleaned areas aren't re-contaminated.
- Dismantle as far as practical to reach internal spaces.
- Leave covers off after cleaning for inspection; record the outcome in the register.
- Get a second opinion; fresh eyes spot missed contamination.
- Optionally add a detergent-based degreaser or disinfectant (examples named in the manual: Septone Truckwash®, Castrol Farmcleanse®, Virkon®). Ask re-sellers for the best product, and remove as much media and plant material as possible before applying.
- If the bay doubles as a chemical wash-down, address all associated OHS issues.

**Designated parking**
- Well sign-posted visitor parking; ideally dedicated nursery vehicles move people around, and other vehicles go straight to visitor parking only.
- Contains new pests away from production; makes it easy to inspect tyres, equipment, floor mats and boots. Monitor this area for new pests.
- Put a biosecurity sign there.
- Don't move nursery machinery through the parking area.

## Summary of key risks and actions (Manual p. 21)

| Risk area | Primary actions |
|---|---|
| Pests | Carry out and record surveillance (prevents establishment, keeps market access). Know key threats. If you suspect a new pest: report to the hotline immediately; restrict people and equipment in the area; wash clothes and skin that touched affected crops, produce or equipment. |
| Product management | Clean, tested inputs and propagation material from reputable suppliers. Record source, testing, treatments and planting location. Collect and dispose of waste away from production. Implement NIASA and BioSecure HACCP. |
| People | Signs at entrances. Visitor register. Provide cleaning equipment; ensure vehicles, clothing and equipment are clean before entry. Manage contractors, utility providers and overseas travellers. |
| Equipment and vehicles | Designated visitor parking. No entry for anyone who won't clean vehicles and equipment. Provide a wash-down facility on site or identify one in the region. |
