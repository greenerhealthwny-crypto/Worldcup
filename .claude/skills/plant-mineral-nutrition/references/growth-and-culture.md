# Growing Plants for Nutrition Research

Distilled from Chapters 2 (Podar), 3 (Schultze), 4 (Gourion et al.), 5 (Moscatiello et al.) and 18 (Berger et al.). The wording is our own. Recipes are reproduced as data (stock concentration, volume per litre, final concentration). Adapt them to local facilities.

---

## §1 Choosing a growth system

| System | Strengths | Weaknesses | Use for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Soil/compost** (peat mix + perlite/vermiculite) | Realistic; easy to grow to seed | Undefined nutrient content; roots hard to recover | Seed bulking; phenotypes under near-normal conditions; ionomics screens |
| **Hydroponics** | Exact, adjustable ion supply; roots accessible; uniform | Needs aeration, pH control, algae control | Deficiency/toxicity treatments; fluxes; root and shoot element analysis |
| **Supported hydroponics** (inert pellets, flood and drain) | Hydroponic control with mechanical support for cereals | More plumbing | Cereal nutrient-use-efficiency studies with imaging (§8) |
| **Agar plates** (½ MS) | Sterile; vertical plates show root architecture | Sucrose and agar artifacts; small plants | Germination; seedling and root phenotypes; producing seedlings for transfer |
| **Cell suspension cultures** | Homogeneous, fast, lots of material | No tissue context | Cellular signalling (Ca²⁺), transport, protoplasts |

**General rules:**
- Use distilled/deionized water (dIH₂O) for watering and solutions. It is **mandatory** if you will analyze minerals.
- Use pots with drainage. Wash reused pots with detergent or ~1 h in bleach.
- The book advises against autoclaving soil, because it changes soil properties.
- If the compost already contains fertilizer, don't add more early on.
- Mix soil 1:1 with vermiculite or perlite (Arabidopsis) to prevent compaction.

## §2 Growth conditions by species (Ch. 2 Notes)

| | Arabidopsis | Barley | Rice |
|---|---|---|---|

| Photoperiod | Long day 16/8 h to flower early. Short day (10/14 h) for large pre-flowering rosettes | 16/8 h | 16/8 or 12/12 h |
| Temperature (day/night) | 20–22 / 17–18 °C. Col-0 is stressed at 23 °C; Ler likes ~23 °C | Cool: 15/10 °C for a full life cycle; 20/15 to 24/18 °C also used; avoid heat | Warm: 25/20 or 27/22 °C (above 20 °C) |
| Relative humidity | High: 60–75% | 60–80% | 40–80% |
| Soil | Peat-based compost pH ~6, 1:1 with vermiculite or perlite | Sphagnum, or compost:perlite 3:1 or 4:1 | Compost:vermiculite or perlite 1:1; avoid clay-loam mineral soil |
| Pots | ~7 × 7 cm cells or 36-cell trays | ~2 L (13–15 cm square minimum) | as barley |
| Hydroponic volume | 5 L box (35×35×20 cm) holds up to 36 plants; holes 1.5–2 cm, 4–5 cm apart | ~1 L per plant to maturity; 5 L holds up to 10 plants; holes 2 cm, ≥5 cm apart | 0.4–1 L per plant (20–24 plants per 8–10 L); holes 2 cm, ≥5 cm apart |
| Solution pH | 5.7–6.5 | 5.5–6.0 | 5.5–6.0 |

**Arabidopsis and other small seeds:**
- Stratify at 4 °C for 2–4 days (3 d suits all ecotypes) for uniform germination.
- Seeds need light, so sow on the surface: from a wet toothpick, or suspended in 0.1% agar and pipetted.
- Cover trays to keep humidity high. Transplant 10–20-day-old seedlings (2–4 leaves).
- Water when the top of the soil is slightly dry (about every 5–7 days). Avoid both waterlogging and drying out.
- For more seed, pinch the first bolt.
- For harvest, stop watering when siliques brown, bag the inflorescence in waxed paper, and collect 7–10 days later. Sieve and store dry at 2–8 °C.
- *Thlaspi* (winter annual): vernalize 6–8-week rosettes at 4 °C for 10 weeks (8/16 h). Don't fertilize until after vernalization. It bolts 7–10 days after returning to long days above 20 °C.

**Barley and rice:**
- Sow about 2.5 cm deep, or germinate on moist filter paper or vermiculite in the dark for 3–5 days (barley below 22 °C, rice at 28 °C).
- Transfer at the first leaf (5–10 cm).
- Feed weekly from week 6–7, for example with a general NPK with trace elements. The book mentions products such as 14:10:27 or 23:4:18 at about 5 g/L.
- Stake plants; harvest heads when brown.

**Pests:** use biological control (predatory mites for thrips), sulfur burners for fungi, and approved insecticides per facility policy. Treat compost 2–4 days before sowing if required.

## §3 Hydroponics: setup and nutrient solutions

**Setup:**
- Use opaque containers to stop algae and protect roots: dark in growth chambers, white in greenhouses to prevent overheating.
- Clear boxes can be painted or taped. Keep aluminium foil out of contact with the solution (Al contamination).
- Support plants in foam, rockwool (pre-soak in solution to equilibrate its pH; it may irritate skin) or cotton plugs about 3 cm tall.
- Sow 2–3 seeds per plug and thin to one, or transplant seedlings with the roots reaching the solution. Sprinkle soil or replace plugs to limit algae.
- **Aerate** continuously with an aquarium pump. **Exception: Arabidopsis.** Agitation from continuous aeration can inhibit root growth, so small unaerated systems (e.g., Magenta boxes) or gentle aeration are used.
- Change the solution every 3–7 days. Check pH and volume, top up for evaporation, and adjust the schedule to plant size.

**Stocks:**
- Make 100–1,000× stocks in dIH₂O with high-purity salts and store at 4 °C.
- Use individual stocks for each macronutrient. Dissolve each micronutrient separately, then combine them into one stock (ZnCl₂ may need a drop of HCl to clear).
- Keep **Fe stock separate and dark.** Discard any stock showing aggregates.
- Adjust pH with KOH (Na-free) or NaOH, and with HCl.

Most research recipes are **modified Hoagland's**. Reduced macronutrient strength prevents osmotic shock; full-strength micronutrients prevent depletion.

### Reference: standard Hoagland No. 2 (Hoagland & Arnon), for orientation
KNO₃ 6 mM, Ca(NO₃)₂ 4 mM, NH₄H₂PO₄ 1 mM, MgSO₄ 2 mM, plus micronutrients and chelated Fe. The book's recipes below are fractions or variants of this.

### Arabidopsis: Gibeaut et al. (1/3 Hoagland macros, full micros)

### Arabidopsis: modified Arteca & Arteca (also works for rice)
- Macronutrients: KNO₃ 1.25 mM, Ca(NO₃)₂ 0.50 mM, MgSO₄ 0.50 mM, KH₂PO₄ 0.625 mM.
- Fe: FeNaEDTA 42.5 µM.
- Micronutrients: Cu 0.16 µM, Zn 0.38 µM, Mn 1.8 µM, B 45 µM, (NH₄)₆Mo₇O₂₄ 0.015 µM, CoCl₂ 0.010 µM.

### *Thlaspi caerulescens* (Zn/Cd hyperaccumulator)
- Macronutrients: KNO₃ 0.5 mM, Ca(NO₃)₂ 0.4 mM, MgSO₄ 0.2 mM, KH₂PO₄ 0.1 mM.
- Fe: FeNaEDTA 20 µM.
- Micronutrients: Cu 0.2 µM, **Zn 12 µM** (high, for a hyperaccumulator), Mn 2 µM, B 10 µM, Na₂MoO₄ 0.1 µM.
- pH 5.5–6.0.

### Spring barley (cv. Golden Promise)
- Macronutrients: KH₂PO₄ 0.4 mM, K₂SO₄ 0.4 mM, MgSO₄ 0.6 mM, NH₄NO₃ 1 mM, Ca(NO₃)₂ 2 mM.
- Fe: FeCl₃ or FeNaEDTA 75 µM.
- Micronutrients: MnCl₂ 7 µM, ZnCl₂ 3 µM, CuSO₄ 0.8 µM, H₃BO₃ 1.6 µM, Na₂MoO₄ 0.83 µM.
- pH 5.5–6.0.

### Rice (after Miyamoto et al.; dilute)
- Macronutrients: (NH₄)₂SO₄ 0.09 mM, KH₂PO₄ 0.05 mM, K₂SO₄ 0.03 mM, MgSO₄ 0.07 mM, KNO₃ 0.05 mM, Ca(NO₃)₂ 0.06 mM.
- Fe: Fe-EDTA 0.11 mM.
- Micronutrients: Mn 1.8 µM, Zn 0.3 µM, Cu 0.3 µM, B 4.6 µM. **No Mo is listed; consider adding it** (e.g., ~0.1 µM molybdate).
- pH 5.5–6.0. The Arteca recipe is a tested alternative.

### Cereals, supported hydroponics (Ch. 18, after Genc et al.)
- 80 L tank; add 400 mL each of stocks A, B and C and 80 mL each of stocks D, E and F:
  - A: NH₄NO₃ 0.04 M + KNO₃ 1 M.
  - B: Ca(NO₃)₂ 0.4 M.
  - C: MgSO₄ 0.4 M + KH₂PO₄ 0.02 M.
  - D: sodium silicate, to a final 0.5 mM.
  - E: NaFe(III)EDTA 0.05 M.
  - F: H₃BO₃ 50 mM, MnCl₂ 5 mM, ZnSO₄ 10 mM, CuSO₄ 0.5 mM, Na₂MoO₄ 0.1 mM.
- pH 6.0 with HCl. Check pH daily; change the solution weekly.
- Plants grow in PVC tubes of 3 mm polycarbonate pellets, flooded and drained every 20 min.
- **Low-N treatments:** swap KNO₃ → KCl and Ca(NO₃)₂ → CaCl₂ to keep K and Ca constant. Don't remove NH₄NO₃ completely: rhizosphere pH drift can cause Fe deficiency.

**Designing deficiency or toxicity treatments:**
- Change one nutrient and hold its counter-ion constant by substitution (e.g., K₂SO₄ ↔ KCl).
- Check the new osmotic strength.
- Use chelated Fe (HBED or EDDHA if pH will rise).
- Buffer if needed (MES 2–2.5 mM at pH 5.5–6.5).
- Remember that seed reserves feed young seedlings, so deficiency takes time to appear.

## §4 Sterile culture on plates

**Media:**
- **½ MS minimal:** half-strength Murashige & Skoog basal salts, no sucrose. 0.8% agar for horizontal plates, 1.5% for vertical.
- **1× MS:** MS salts, Gamborg's B5 vitamins (filter-sterilized, added after autoclaving), 2.56 mM MES-KOH pH 5.7, 1% sucrose (from filter-sterilized 20% stock, added after autoclaving), 1.5% agar.
- **Sucrose trade-off:** it makes germination more uniform but invites fungi and alters physiology. Healthy seeds don't need it.

**Pouring:**
1. Dissolve the salts in 900 mL, adjust pH to 5.6–6.0 with KOH, **then** add agar, and make up to volume (allow for later additions).
2. Autoclave 121 °C, 20 min.
3. Pour in a laminar-flow hood and dry the plates for 30–60 min, the same time every batch.
4. Store upside down at 4 °C for up to 1–2 weeks.
5. Seal plates with **Micropore tape** (breathable, less condensation), not Parafilm.

**Surface sterilization of seeds** (in a laminar-flow hood; autoclave all transgenic waste):
- **Small seeds, few lots:**
  1. 1 mL 96–100% ethanol for 1 min (never more than 2 min).
  2. 30–50% household bleach for **10 min maximum** (longer lowers germination).
  3. Spin briefly and wash 2–3× in sterile water.
  - Alternative: 70% ethanol 5 min, then 50% bleach + 0.02% Triton X-100 for 5 min, then 4 washes.
- **Many lots: chlorine gas.**
  1. Open tubes in a rack inside a sealed box **in a fume hood**, with 100 mL bleach in a beaker.
  2. Add 3 mL concentrated HCl and close immediately.
  3. Leave about 4 h (never over 24 h).
  4. Vent 10 min.
  - Label tubes in pencil; chlorine removes ink.
- **Large seeds (cereals):**
  1. Dehusk if they will be used for transformation or long in vitro culture.
  2. 1 min in 96–100% ethanol (or 3 min in 70%).
  3. 15–20 min in 20% bleach (~1% NaOCl) + 0.02–0.05% Tween-20, stirred, using 1.5–2× the seed volume.
  4. Wash **5–8×** in sterile water; leftover bleach impairs germination.
- Stratify at 4 °C for 2–3 days (before or after plating; wrap plates in foil). Sow small seeds from 0.1% sterile agar with a pipette tip.
- Grow **vertically** for root measurements and easy transfer.

## §5 Arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) symbiosis (Ch. 3; *Medicago truncatula*)

**Why:** more than 90% of plant species form AM symbioses. The fungi (Glomeromycota) can supply most of the plant's P, and they are a lever for sustainable crop nutrition.

**Key rule: keep phosphate low.** High P, or slow growth that lets P accumulate, suppresses colonization.

**Glasshouse protocol:**
1. Scarify seeds lightly and pre-germinate for 1 day on wet paper in the dark.
2. Substrate: 4 vol coarse sand + 5 vol calcined clay (washed free of nutrients) + 1 vol commercial AM inoculum (granular *Rhizophagus/Glomus* mixes work well and cheaply). Use trays with ~50 mL compartments.
3. Plant **one plant per compartment** for mutant screens. Fungi spreading from neighbouring wild-type "nurse" plants can colonize mutants and make them look normal.
4. Grow 4–6 weeks. Water with deionized water. From the 1–2 trifoliate-leaf stage, feed weekly with a **low-P** solution (modified Rorison R solution, pH 6.5).

**Checking colonization:**
1. Clear root pieces in 10% KOH (Medicago: 3–4 days at room temperature, or about 20 min at 90 °C).
2. Rinse, then stain 10–20 min in **5% fountain-pen ink + 5% acetic acid** (the ink-vinegar method).
3. Destain in 1% acetic acid.
4. Score % root length colonized (gridline intersect method) under a microscope.
5. Sample only part of the root system if the plants will be kept for seed.

**Spores and inoculum:**
- Isolate spores by wet sieving: 100 mL soil in 1 L water, sieves from 1 mm down to 40 µm, then centrifugation on 45% sucrose (keep the contact brief to avoid osmotic damage).
- Multiply inoculum on leek in pot culture for about 6 months.
- Surface-sterilize spores: 2% chloramine-T + Tween (2 × 15 min, 4 °C), then streptomycin 0.02% + gentamicin 0.01%.

**Root organ culture (axenic):**
- Carrot hairy roots transformed with *Agrobacterium rhizogenes*, grown on M/MW media (low P; sucrose; Phytagel; pH 5.5) in two-compartment plates.
- The fungus grows into the sugar-free hyphal compartment. A filter-paper bridge helps hyphae cross.
- Refreshing the media can yield up to about 75,000 spores per plate.
- Seal plates with PVC or LDPE food wrap rather than Parafilm (better gas exchange; Parafilm can release toxic compounds).

## §6 Rhizobial nodulation assays (Ch. 4; *M. truncatula* with *Sinorhizobium meliloti*)

**Key rule: combined N suppresses nodulation.** Assay under N-free or N-limited conditions, but start seedlings with a little N so they don't starve before the symbiosis forms.

**Plant lines and strains:**
- *M. truncatula* R108 and Jemalong (line J5).
- Rm41 nodulates R108 efficiently but is **Fix⁻ on Jemalong**.
- Rm1021 is the classic strain for Jemalong, but it fixes N poorly on both lines.
- Match strain to genotype.

**Greenhouse assay:**
1. Seeds: scarify (sandpaper or concentrated H₂SO₄), sterilize, and hold 2 days at 4 °C in the dark for synchronous germination.
2. Substrate: **sterilized sand:perlite.** It aerates well and washes off roots; vermiculite sticks to them.
3. Feed:
   - First, an N-rich solution: commercial NPK 18-6-26 with trace elements at 0.8 g/L, about 10 mM NO₃⁻.
   - Then the **N-limited solution**: 0.125 mM KNO₃, 0.125 mM KH₂PO₄, 0.31 mM CaCl₂, 0.062 mM MgSO₄, 0.156 mM K₂SO₄, 14 µM FeNaEDTA, and B/Mn/Zn/Mo/Cu micronutrients.
   - If plants show starvation (red stems), switch to the N-poor solution only.
4. Conditions: 16 h day, 22/16 °C day/night, 60% relative humidity, about 200 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹. Red light in the spectrum helps Medicago.
5. Water gently from above in week 1, then from below (capillary uptake) to keep leaves dry. Don't keep the substrate permanently wet.
6. Inoculate with bacteria grown in rich TY medium, then **resuspended in water** (removes medium compounds that trigger defenses). Treat all seedlings the same way.

**In vitro assay:**
- Use **BNM (buffered nodulation medium)**: 2 mM CaSO₄, 2 mM MES, pH 6.5, plus Mg, P, micronutrients (with Co) and FeEDTA, and no N.
- Use a specific agar brand; *M. truncatula* is very sensitive to agar type, and the wrong one arrests roots and blocks nodulation.
- Add **0.5 µM AVG** (an ethylene-synthesis inhibitor; heat-labile, so add to cooled medium) for reproducible nodulation.
- Shade roots from light.

**Hairy-root composite plants** (*A. rhizogenes*, kanamycin selection) let you test transgenes in roots quickly. Sterilize transgenic bacterial suspensions with hypochlorite before disposal.

## §7 Plant cell suspension cultures and protoplasts (Ch. 5)

- **Basics:** sterile technique throughout. MS or Gamborg B5 salts + vitamins + sucrose + hormones (an auxin such as 2,4-D, with or without cytokinin). About 24 °C, pH 5.5–5.8, shaking.
- **Initiating a culture:** sterile seedling explants → callus on agar medium (subculture every ~4 weeks; callus is a slow-growing backup line) → break a large enough callus piece into liquid medium.
- **Flask volume:** fill flasks to **one-fifth** of their volume for aeration (20 mL in 100 mL, 50 in 250, 100 in 500). Subculture about 1 mL packed cell volume into 20 mL fresh medium at regular intervals. Optimize sucrose and hormone ratios over 3–4 subcultures.
- **Protoplasts:**
  - Digest with cellulase and pectinase in an isoosmotic buffer (keep dark; light-sensitive).
  - Filter through nylon mesh.
  - Handle gently: centrifuge brake off, wide-bore tips.
  - Count with a haemocytometer. Check viability with Trypan blue (dead cells stain blue) or FDA.
- **Regeneration:** by organogenesis (hormone balance: high auxin:cytokinin gives roots, low gives shoots) or by somatic embryogenesis. Cultured plant cells are **totipotent**, which underpins transformation.
- **Differences from animal cell culture:** 24 °C vs 37 °C; sucrose vs glucose; pH 5.5–5.8 vs 7.2–7.4; light; plant cells can divide indefinitely.

## §8 Pot experiments and image-based phenotyping of nutrient use efficiency (Ch. 18)

**Why:** mineral fertilizer accounts for about 30–60% of crop yield. Better agronomy plus nutrient-efficient cultivars can raise yield without more fertilizer. NDVI sensors work on closed field canopies. In the greenhouse, **colour imaging of individual plants** measures growth rate and greenness (an indicator of N status) non-destructively over time.

**Watering pots correctly:**
- "Pot capacity" (free-draining) depends on pot height and can leave fine mixes hypoxic.
- Instead, measure **field capacity at 1 m suction**:
  1. Put the mix in a sintered-glass funnel whose outlet tubing runs to a water reservoir 1 m below.
  2. Equilibrate for several days (no air bubbles; cover to stop evaporation).
  3. Weigh wet (WW), then oven-dry at 105 °C and weigh dry (DW).
  4. (WW − DW)/DW is the **gravimetric** water content at field capacity. Multiply by bulk density for volumetric.
- Fill all pots to the same dry weight, mix nutrients in (about 10 mL of each stock per kg dry soil; a cement mixer works for big batches), and **water to weight** daily or every second day.
- Include spare unplanted pots to track evaporation.
- Soil:sand or peat:sand mixes resist compaction on conveyor belts. Pure sand holds too little water.

**Imaging:**
- Use uniform seedlings. Sow extra and stagger sowing if lines germinate at different rates.
- Take 2 side views (90° apart) plus 1 top view, at 15–20 time points. That is about 240 MB per plant, so plan storage.
- Keep lighting even (warm up the lamps first). Put a **colour reference card** (e.g., Munsell plant-tissue chart) and a ruler in frame to calibrate colour and scale.
- Background, pot and stake colours must contrast with green: white or blue, never green or grey. White pots keep soil cooler. Cover soil with white gravel or coloured mulch so it isn't mistaken for senescent leaves.
- **Outputs:** projected shoot area (which correlates with biomass), growth curves, relative growth rate, and leaf colour or greenness (estimates chlorophyll and N status). Compare genotypes under low vs adequate nutrient supply to rank nutrient use efficiency. This can support forward-genetics screens.
