# High-Protein Meal Prep (Stealth Health method)

Built from the user's copy of the *Stealth Health Meal Prep Cookbook* (2024) by Tom Walsh (@Stealth_Health_Life): "high-protein meal preps that taste like cheat meals". The skill captures the method, the swap system and the recipe index. It does not reproduce the full recipes; for an exact recipe, read it from the user's Google Doc (see "Getting an exact recipe").

## Core philosophy (from the book)
- The best diet is one you enjoy; the less it feels like dieting, the better.
- Nothing has to be cut out. Weight loss comes from a calorie deficit; one way to eat fewer calories without eating less is to eat lower-calorie versions of foods you love.
- Meal prep only works if you genuinely look forward to it, i.e., it has to beat the burger at the food court. Plain chicken, rice and broccoli fails.

## The method

**1. The leftover approach (not a dreaded Sunday prep day).** Two or three times a week, whenever you have energy, cook dinner as a big batch. Cooking has *fixed costs* (shopping, setup, boiling water, cooking rice/pasta, roasting veg, cleanup, dishes) that barely change with quantity, and small *variable costs* (juicing 4 limes instead of 1). So make about 10 servings instead of 1–2. Most book recipes make 5–6; once you know you like one, double it. Portion into equal servings right away, eat one, freeze the rest. Over time the freezer becomes your own "frozen food aisle".

**2. Freeze everything.** The author recommends freezer over fridge for every recipe in the book; in his side-by-side test a meal frozen for a month beat one cooked a day earlier and refrigerated. Tips:
- Let food cool before covering and freezing, or freeze uncovered ~30 minutes, then cover.
- The more airtight, the longer it lasts; he uses paper or glass meal prep containers with tight lids and reports no issues at 2–3 months (burritos: at least 2–3 months, kept 6+ months without issues).
- Food-safety framing: frame "never go bad" as the author's experience. Freezing stops microbial growth but doesn't kill everything, and quality slowly declines, so label containers with date and contents, and reheat until steaming hot. The book itself cooks chicken to an internal 165 °F.

**3. Reheating.**
- *Bowls:* loosen the lid or cover with a paper towel, microwave 2 min, stir, microwave another 2.5–3 min. If dry, add 1–2 Tbsp milk or water to bring frozen sauces back.
- *Burritos:* wrap tightly in foil or parchment (tape parchment), optionally inside an airtight zip bag. Reheat from frozen (no thaw): wrap in a paper towel, microwave 2–3 min to thaw rapidly without fully cooking, then air fry at 375 °F for 6–8 min to crisp (or pan fry 4–5 min per side with a little butter/oil). Microwave only: 4–5 min flipping halfway, then rest 4–5 min.

**4. The 500-calorie meal prep (the book's centrepiece).** Set a *tracked* calorie baseline with 1–3 meal preps of ~500 kcal a day, then intuitively track the rest. The book's argument: people who estimate intake under-report by more than 20% on average; if your target is 2,000 kcal, estimating all of it at 20% error means ~400 kcal/day of unseen overeating, versus ~200 kcal/day if two meals are fixed 500 kcal preps. That 200 kcal/day gap is ~72,800 kcal/year, about 21 lb of fat at the book's 3,500 kcal/lb rule of thumb. Present these as the book's illustrative numbers, not personal guarantees.

## The swap system (how "stealth health" recipes are built)
Use these when designing new recipes or lightening a user's favourite. All are patterns used repeatedly in the book.

- **Blended cottage-cheese sauces** replace cream/butter sauces: low-fat (2% or fat-free) cottage cheese blended until completely smooth (1–2 min) with a little 1% milk and a flavour-dense cheese. Variants:
  - *Alfredo:* cottage cheese + milk or fat-free evaporated milk + freshly grated Parmigiano Reggiano (fresh, not pre-grated, which may not emulsify) + salt, pepper, garlic.
  - *Mac & cheese:* cottage cheese + reduced-fat cheddar/mozzarella + cheddar powder (dehydrated cheddar, e.g., packets from boxed mac) + a little nutritional yeast + milk + flavour (Cajun, buffalo, BBQ, jalapeño popper, green chile, gochujang, truffle).
  - *Flavoured creams:* blend in roasted red peppers, roasted butternut squash, chipotle in adobo, pesto and basil, marinara/arrabbiata, pickled jalapeños.
  - Pour blended sauce into the pan the protein was cooked in **with the heat off** (pan not too hot) to pick up the fond without splitting; thin with reserved pasta water.
- **Fat-free evaporated milk** (not condensed) as a cream base for Alfredo and pan sauces.
- **Greek yogurt (0–2%) or skyr** for marinades (tandoori, shawarma, butter chicken), white/garlic sauces, chipotle cream, ranch, queso bases and burrito fillings; often cut with light mayo for creaminess.
- **Light/one-third-fat cream cheese, Laughing Cow wedges, reduced- and fat-free cheeses** layered (e.g., fat-free cheddar under, 2% cheddar on top for melt).
- **Lean proteins:** chicken breast (tenderised/flattened, thin-sliced) and boneless skinless thighs; 93–96% lean ground beef; skirt steak or top sirloin trimmed of exterior fat; turkey or chicken sausage; center-cut bacon in small amounts for big flavour; turkey pepperoni; egg whites plus a few whole eggs.
- **Flavour without fat:** dry rubs and marinades (soy sauce, citrus, garlic, chili crisp, gochujang, adobo, hot sauce, Cajun/taco/fajita/ranch packets), charring and roasting veg, caramelising onions with water or bone broth instead of oil, a little honey or zero-calorie brown sugar for balance, acids (lemon, lime, white wine vinegar) to finish.
- **Measured fats:** oils and butter in weighed small amounts (often 10–30 g per batch); light butter.
- **Carbs by weight:** pasta, orzo, basmati/jasmine rice weighed dry; protein pasta (Barilla Protein+, Banza) optional; day-old cooked rice (~300 g dry ≈ 700 g cooked) for fried rice.
- **Freezer-friendly eggs:** whisk a cornstarch slurry into egg whites before cooking so they survive freezing and reheating; steam-cook in the microwave in 45-second bursts, stopping at 80–90% set.
- **Beef tenderising ("velveting"):** thin-slice partially frozen beef, soak ~15 min in water with a little baking soda.

## Standard building blocks
- **Rice:** rinse in a mesh strainer under cold water ~2 minutes; boil with water at about 1.5× the dry rice weight (e.g., 300 g rice : 450 g water); once boiling, cover on low 15 min, then off heat covered 10 min more. Swap water for broth, soup or add butter/bouillon for flavour.
- **Air-fried chicken:** season evenly (mix equal parts of each spice in bulk and coat both sides), air fry 375–400 °F for about 9–15 min depending on cut and thickness (thighs ~12–15 min at 375 °F), rest 5 min, then dice. Cook in batches if needed.
- **Assembly:** cook protein, carb and sauce separately; combine; divide evenly by weight into the stated number of containers.
- **Burritos:** about 150–160 g filling per tortilla (weigh it); let fillings cool (some recipes chill the filling 30–45 min to thicken) before rolling tightly. Standard burrito tortillas, or high-protein tortillas (the author uses Counter brand) to raise protein.

## Kitchen kit the book assumes
Air fryer, blender (high-speed or bullet; immersion works), oven, microwave, and a **food scale** (recipes are in grams and ounces so batches split evenly).

## How to run it
1. Find out the goal (calorie target, protein target, meals per day to prep), dietary limits (allergies, no pork, vegetarian, dairy-free, etc.), equipment, budget, and cuisine preferences. Ask once for missing essentials, otherwise assume and say so.
2. Pick the job:
   - *Weekly or monthly prep plan:* choose recipes from `references/recipe-index.md` to hit the targets; use the leftover approach (2–3 cook nights) rather than one marathon day; give a freezer rotation and a consolidated grocery list grouped by store section, scaled to the number of batches.
   - *Lighten a recipe the user loves:* keep its signature flavours, apply the swap system, aim for ~500 kcal and ~35–50 g protein per serving, give gram weights and servings.
   - *Design a new recipe in this style:* protein + carb + blended or yogurt-based sauce + bold seasoning + garnish; batch for 5–10 servings.
   - *Exact book recipe:* see below.
3. **Macros:** use the book's printed numbers for book recipes. For new or modified recipes, label nutrition as an estimate, show the main ingredient assumptions, and tell the user to check with a nutrition app using their exact brands (macros vary a lot with tortillas, cheese and pasta brands).
4. End with at most one line offering a next step (grocery list, freezer label sheet, doubling a batch, a burrito version of a bowl).

## Quality rules
- Taste first: every plan or recipe must be something the user would look forward to eating.
- Give quantities by weight where it matters (protein, carbs, cheese, sauces) and the number of servings.
- Don't present estimated macros as exact; never give medical or clinical nutrition advice. For medical diets, pregnancy, eating disorders or kidney issues, suggest a dietitian.
- Respect allergies and restrictions absolutely (peanut sauce, cashews in butter chicken, fish sauce, dairy everywhere).
- Keep food-safety basics: cool before freezing, airtight containers, labelled with date, reheat until steaming hot, chicken cooked to 165 °F.
- Credit the source when quoting book recipes; don't paste whole recipes into shareable/public outputs.
