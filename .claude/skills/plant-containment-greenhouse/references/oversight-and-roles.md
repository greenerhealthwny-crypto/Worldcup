# Regulation, Oversight and Roles (2008 snapshot)

Source: Guide Sections I–II (pp. 1–10). Everything here reflects US rules as of 2008. Agencies, programs, permit types, phone numbers and URLs have changed since; tell the user to confirm current requirements with their IBC/BSO and the agency. The guide itself said greenhouse research is generally not subject to federal regulation, but field tests, movement and commercialization are, guidance is case by case, and you should consult agencies very early.

## NIH Guidelines and Appendix P
- First published 1976 for rDNA research. Advisory, not law, but federal funders require compliance as a condition of funding; non-compliance risks suspension or termination of rDNA funding at the institution.
- Cover risk assessment and containment for four levels plus exempt experiments; extended over time to plant, animal and human gene therapy work.
- **Appendix P** (1994): physical and biological containment for rDNA research with plants. "Plants" includes mosses, liverworts, macroscopic algae and vascular plants (crop, forest, weed, ornamental). Also covers plant-associated microorganisms (viroids, virusoids, viruses, bacteria, fungi, protozoa, and benign/beneficial ones like some *Rhizobium*; plus microbes modified to associate with plants) and plant-associated small animals: arthropods obligately associated with plants, plant pests, pollinators, disease vectors, and others like nematodes needing plants for testing, plus their microbes.
- Plants in the lab (growth chambers, tissue culture rooms, open benches) fall under **Appendix G**.

## US federal agencies (Coordinated Framework)
- **USDA-APHIS:** authority under the Plant Protection Act to protect agriculture from pests and disease, extended to rDNA plants and potential plant pests; follows International Plant Protection Convention standards.
  - **PPQ** (Plant Protection and Quarantine): entry, establishment and spread of pests and noxious weeds.
  - **BRS** (Biotechnology Regulatory Services): GE organisms that may pose plant health risks. Also plant-made veterinary biologics.
  - In 2008, any "introduction" (import, interstate movement, environmental release) of a plant pest or GEO needed a Notification or permit. APHIS didn't regulate use inside contained facilities or judge their adequacy, but unauthorized release (survivable material outside containment) was a violation. It could inspect any facility receiving interstate or imported regulated material; inspected before the first shipment and every 2–3 years under movement/release permits; withheld permits until fixes were made.
  - You may need several APHIS permits (e.g., BRS plus a PPQ 526 or 588); a BRS permit doesn't replace PPQ permits or vice versa. The guide warned BRS rules were about to change to a tiered permit system.
- **EPA** (Office of Pesticide Programs, Biopesticides and Pollution Prevention Division): GE microbial pesticides and plant-incorporated protectants (e.g., Bt proteins in plants).
- **FDA:** GE foods, feed, additives, human and veterinary drugs; doesn't regulate R&D, but developers are expected to consult during development.
- **CDC (with APHIS):** National Select Agent Registry, jointly administered since 2002 legislation; eight plant pathogen Select Agents listed in 2008 (check the current list).

Table 1 (examples of overlapping oversight, 2008):

| Trait | Example | Agencies |
|---|---|---|
| Virus resistance, food crop | PRSV-resistant papaya | USDA, EPA, FDA |
| Insect resistance, food crop | Bt maize | USDA, EPA, FDA |
| Herbicide tolerance, ornamental | Glyphosate-tolerant marigold | USDA, EPA, FDA |
| Herbicide tolerance, food crop | Glyphosate-tolerant maize | USDA, EPA, FDA |
| Agronomic trait | High-laurate canola | USDA, FDA |
| Phytoremediation | Transgenic poplar | USDA, EPA |
| Plant-made pharmaceutical | Antibody-producing *Lemna* | USDA, FDA |
| Transgenic insect | GFP pink bollworm | USDA, EPA (some cases) |
| Select Agent plant pathogen | Huanglongbing (citrus greening) agents | USDA or CDC |

USDA: agriculture and environment. FDA: food and feed safety. EPA: environment, PIP food/feed safety, companion herbicides.

## International
- **IPPC** (International Plant Protection Convention) treaty on preventing pest spread; phytosanitary standards, reporting and information. Regional organizations under it include **NAPPO** (US via APHIS, Canada via CFIA, Mexico) and **EPPO** (European and Mediterranean, about 50 countries).
- Outside the US, map these roles to the national plant protection organization and biosafety regulator.

## Local roles
- **Institutional Biosafety Committee (IBC):** required at federally funded institutions doing transgenic research. At least five members, two unaffiliated "citizen members" (e.g., local officials, state environmental staff, medical/occupational/environmental professionals), and at least one with plant, plant pathogen or plant pest containment expertise. Keeps/verifies rDNA research records; NIH contact point; reviews proposals and the PI's proposed level (unmodified organism first, then effect of the modification); evaluates facilities, procedures and personnel expertise; adopts emergency plans for breaches; may set closeout policies with written project termination dates.
- **Biological Safety Officer (BSO):** must be appointed for BL3-P or BL4-P work or large-scale microbial research. Sits on the IBC, liaises between researchers and IBC, develops emergency plans, inspects facilities and protocols.
- **Containment or quarantine officer:** required for USDA-inspected containment facilities holding permitted material. Runs and maintains the facility day to day; often writes and implements SOPs, enforces guidelines, responds to security or breach issues, trains people, handles permitted packages. Can be a researcher, PI, BSO, greenhouse manager, permittee or unit director.
- **Principal Investigator (PI):** ultimately responsible for the project and biosafety compliance; trains and supervises staff; communicates with IBC, BSO, regulators, greenhouse manager and staff; corrects anything that could breach containment; proposes the containment level and protocols; responsible for all APHIS-regulated material; files notification with the IBC for all GE plant experiments (timing depends on level; some need further approvals per NIH Section III).
- **Greenhouse staff:** from part-time waterers to skilled trades. All should know the containment requirements of ongoing work; a short orientation on the research, organisms and special procedures usually suffices. Manager and PI ensure compliance.

## Audience the guide addresses
Greenhouse managers, facility staff, regulators, research scientists and students, IBC members, designers working on retrofits or new builds, and tradespeople, maintenance staff and neighbors who benefit from understanding why containment matters.
