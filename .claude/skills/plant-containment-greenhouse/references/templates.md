# Templates and Checklists

Source: Guide Appendices I–III and Section V. Adapt to the user's institution; these are outlines, not official forms. Appendix IV (APHIS-PPQ SOP outline), Appendix V (resources), the glossary and reference list were not in the extracted text; see the PDF.

## 1. Biosafety review form (for IBC/BSO) — outline of Appendix I
Sample courtesy of the Donald Danforth Plant Science Center. Mark "n/a" where not applicable. The PI is responsible for staff safety training on the project.

**Header:** date; IBC review required (Y/N); project title; group; investigator (name, highest degree, title); other personnel; locations where materials will be used.

**Protocol:** a paragraph on intent and objectives (no proprietary detail needed).

**Classification (check all that apply, complete each matching section):** rDNA molecules (A); plants incl. rDNA hosts (B); plant pathogens and symbiotes (C); field tests (D); >10 L large-scale culture (E); non-primate animal systems (F); human-derived materials (G); animal pathogens (H); vertebrate toxins and toxin expression (I).

**General items:** related projects (send the review to outside labs receiving material); lab-related illnesses in the past year; containment and safety procedures; solid and liquid biological waste disposal; emergency procedures for spills and exposures with contacts; medical surveillance needed?; hazard communication; proposed containment level (use NIH Appendices G and P). Changes in quantity, material or facilities may trigger re-review.

**Section A, rDNA:** prokaryotic vector systems (type, host, vector source, synthetic/natural, conjugative or not); viral systems (description, host); plastid systems; yeast systems (host, vector, episomal or integrated); nature of inserted DNA; scale (quantity, frequency, manner of use); exempt activities (IBC notification still required).

**Section B, plants (complete Section A too for rDNA plants):** species; rDNA transposable elements present; reproduction mode (asexual, open pollination, selfing, apomixis); potential pollen release in the work area; known pollinosis, contact dermatitis or other effects; modified traits; invasiveness/weediness; propagation locations; containment level (BL1-P to BL4-P); safety practices for controlling undesired species and motile macro-organisms; lab, growth chamber and greenhouse design.

**Section C, pathogens and symbiotes:** type (insect, nematode, fungal, bacterial, viral); pertinent information; APHIS permit numbers if acquired under permit; containment level; specific handling rules.

**Sign-offs:** PI agreement to follow NIH Guidelines and regulatory requirements for use, shipment and transfer; IBC chair approval and date; space for BSO/reviewers and a reference number.

## 2. Sample pathogen quarantine guideline — TMV (Appendix II)
Courtesy of Purdue University. Example of a practical in-house protocol for a contact-spread virus. Header field: "Infected plants to be disposed by: ____".
1. After working with infected plants, don't enter other growing areas; plan tasks accordingly.
2. Wear latex gloves and lab coats (or disposable sleeves) when touching plants; change gloves between plants. Alternatively dip hands in milk (whole or skim).
3. Spray plants with milk 24 hours before handling. Smokers dip hands in milk before handling.
4. Avoid contact with sleeves, tools, hose nozzles or anything that can carry TMV to other plants.
5. Space/prune plants so they don't touch or get brushed during routine work; keep new plants physically separate.
6. Greenshield or 3% trisodium phosphate (TSP) are the most effective tool/surface disinfectants; 70% ethanol is not effective.
7. Greenshield and TSP are not safe for hands; milk inactivates TMV and is safe.
8. Greenhouse staff autoclave discarded plants, even non-transgenic ones.
9. Plant owners manage TMV spread the same way in other growth areas they use.
10. Propagate infected lines by meristem culture (perhaps with heat treatment); other vegetative propagation spreads the virus. Acid- or bleach-treat seed to remove TMV.

## 3. Biotechnology facility inspection worksheet — APHIS-BRS style (Appendix III)
Non-exhaustive sample questions an inspector may ask. Answers Yes/No/N/A; every No or N/A is explained in a Summary of Findings (SOF). Inspector answers from discussion, documents and observation, and photographs labs, chambers, greenhouses and storage.

**General**
1. Do SOPs set proper handling techniques for transgenic material?
2. Was the inspector given a copy of the SOPs?
3. Is there an IBC? (chair name and phone in SOF)
4. Does the IBC chair know the work involves USDA-regulated material?
5. Is the researcher listed on the permit application? (list researchers in SOF)
6. Does the researcher have the permit application, SOPs and BRS contact info?
7. Do SOPs give detailed instructions for reporting and correcting unintended release?
8. Are other project staff trained per written SOPs?
9. Are training records kept?
10. Will regulated material move within the facility? (list rooms/greenhouses/chamber serial numbers)
11. Were floor plans showing the research areas provided?

**Security and prevention of commingling**
12. Is the general area secure from public access?
13. Can individual labs be locked?
14. Are rooms signed "USDA Regulated Material" or "Genetically Engineered Organisms"?
15. "Authorized Personnel Only" signs posted?
16. Lockable cabinets/storage for all regulated material (seed, tissue culture, microbes)?
17. Are those storage areas identified? (if No to 14, 15 or 17, give a posting date)
18. Will regulated material be color-coded and/or labeled?
19. Are labels clear and durable? (see an example)
20. Do SOPs cover cleanup/disposal of spilled seed or material?
21. Do SOPs cover cleaning or disposal of equipment and PPE so nothing is released?
22. Do SOPs specify devitalization and disposal at the end? (methods in SOF)
23. Will sexually compatible non-transgenic species be absent from research areas for the whole trial?
24. If not, is labeling enough to keep them segregated?
25. If not, will transgenic plants be kept from sexual maturity for the whole trial?
26. Are receipt, propagation and destruction logs kept?

**Laboratory**
27. Does the work use labs?
28. Will material be grown or germinated in the lab?
29. Is the area free of cracks or irregular surfaces that trap seed?
30. Free of places seed can be lost or lodged?
31. Is work done in a biosafety cabinet or hood?
32. Is the lab free of drains?
33. If not, do drains go to a special waste trap?
34. Are drains screened to suit the material?
35. Are disposal/devitalization methods for collected material in the SOPs?

**Growth chamber**
36. Does the work use growth chambers?
37. Lockable?
38. Dedicated to transgenic material?
39. Is this the only work in the chamber?
40. Will plants/microbes be kept from sexual maturity?
41. If not, does the venting/HVAC likely stop pollen/spores leaving the facility?
42. Free of drains?
43. If not, do drains go to a special waste trap?
44. Drains screened appropriately?
45. Disposal/devitalization methods in SOPs?

**Greenhouse**
46. Does the work use greenhouses?
47. Authorized personnel only?
48. Does the greenhouse manager know it's USDA-regulated material?
49. Does the manager know the permit conditions (SOPs, standard and supplemental conditions)?
50. Do all doors and alternate exits lock?
51. Is there a double-door entry or headhouse?
52. Will plants be kept from sexual maturity?
53. If not, will flowers be bagged or removed?
54. If not, does the vent/exhaust/HVAC system likely stop pollen/seed/spores leaving?
55. Will sexually compatible non-transgenic species be absent for the whole trial?
56. If not, is labeling enough to segregate?
57. Will soil be reused? (treatment or disposal method in SOF)
58. Do vents open manually?
59. Do vents open automatically?
60. If so, is there an override to stop automatic opening?
61. Are vents screened to stop most insects?
62. Are exhaust fans enclosed in screen structures?
63. Do exhaust fan louvers work (close when fans stop, lubricated, intact)?
64. Is screening intact with no noticeable holes or gaps?
65. Do walls, floors and doors exclude rodents and other vectors?
66. Sticky board/tape insect traps?
67. Black-light traps?
68. Other traps for insect or rodent pollen/seed vectors? (list in SOF)

## 4. Minimum in-house inspection questions (per room or project, from Section V)
- Who are the responsible parties and how are they reached?
- What is the experiment and how is it identified?
- What containment level is prescribed, and do the facilities meet it?
- Which physical and biological measures achieve containment?
- Are SOPs available and followed?
- Any evidence of containment deficiencies?
- How is the area secured, and what security is required?
- Is there a written loss-of-containment plan?
- What is the most likely cause of a breach?
- How will materials be disposed of at the end?

## 5. SOP topic list (APHIS-permitted facilities)
Use, maintain and disinfect facility and equipment; respond to emergencies; maintain security; manage visitors; handle a containment breach; replace glazing; plus receiving and opening permitted packages, internal movement of material, termination/disposal with validation, pest control, monitoring and traps, training and records. Review at least annually and whenever permits, practices, equipment or staff change.
