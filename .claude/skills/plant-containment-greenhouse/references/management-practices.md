# Management Practices

Source: Guide Section V (pp. 27–36). Containment only works when staff understand and follow procedures. Practices should fit the assigned level; excessive ones discourage compliance. Committed staff are the first to notice unusual conditions.

## Access
- Restricted at every level to limit pollen, seed and propagules carried between rooms. Public visits discouraged or banned.
- BL1-P: at manager/PI discretion while experiments run. BL2-P: limited to people directly involved. BL3-P: manager with PI authorizes individuals. Maintenance staff get discretionary access; interested visitors are escorted.
- Entry may be through a vestibule (interlocking doors, light traps, air wash). Some high-containment programs require exit via shower/change room.
- One large undivided greenhouse may force restricting the whole facility; give authorized people keys or cards.
- Post access signs (with access instructions if useful).
- Entry/exit logbook required only at BL4-P, but APHIS recommended logging regular staff, visitors and service personnel (names, dates, times in and out) whenever exotic infectious agents are present.

## Apparel and hygiene
- BL1-P/BL2-P: street or lab clothes; lab coats kept at the facility recommended and often required. No backpacks, coats or purses without good reason (pests hitchhike). Watch footwear. No eating, drinking or smoking. Gloves encouraged on entry or when handling live material; wash hands on exit.
- BL3-P: disposable gowns, gloves, caps/hair nets and/or shoe covers usually required; removed before leaving and decontaminated (usually autoclaved) before washing or disposal.
- BSL-3Ag/BL4-P: enter only via dressing/shower rooms; remove street clothes, wear protective clothing; remove it and shower when leaving. Shower on entry only if outside organisms could contaminate the work. Store clothing in the inner change room and autoclave before laundering; autoclave or incinerate disposables.

## Signage and labeling
- APHIS required restricted-access signs for material under APHIS permit ("USDA Regulated Material"); exact wording comes from the permit.
- BL1-P: no special signs. BL2-P and up: "authorized personnel only". If organisms threaten the local ecosystem or agriculture, a sign saying so on access doors, with the responsible person's name and phone, plants in use and special requirements; may add emergency contacts and a non-confidential description of the risk.
- Don't let signs undermine security; omit personal contact details if that's a security concern and regulation allows.
- Reserve the biohazard symbol for infectious agents that threaten people; overuse desensitizes.
- Emergency exits follow building code; under APHIS permit: "USDA-APHIS Containment Facility—Emergency Exit Only".
- Mark transgenic material to distinguish it from controls and other plants. If permitted GEOs share a room with a non-transgenic variety of the same species, separate them in space and/or flowering time.
- Give GEOs a marked bench boundary (e.g., color-coded markers); label pots, bench sections or benches with plant and modification (e.g., "Soybeans with viral coat protein gene"). Barcodes track plants and can carry regulatory data.
- Everything in a room is handled at the highest level any material in it requires.

## Storage and handling
- Lockable coolers, freezers and growth chambers recommended.
- Transgenic seed in a locked, labeled cabinet, ideally inside a greenhouse room. Cabinets holding APHIS-permitted material must be signed.
- Seed handled outside containment (headhouse cabinet, potting bench) goes in spill-proof containers. Prevent germination in unwanted places.
- Threshers, seed counters etc. must be easy to clean; dedicate equipment if mixing between runs is a risk. Decontaminate all waste and unused seed appropriately.

## Transfer of materials
- BL2-P and up: seeds, propagules, potted plants, seedling trays moved in closed, non-breakable containers.
- BL3-P/BL4-P: also inside a sealed secondary container; decontaminate its exterior (chemically or in a fumigation chamber) if the same plant, host or vector is within the organism's dissemination distance.
- Open incoming packages in a biosafety cabinet or sleeved cage inside containment, then move material into tissue culture, chambers or greenhouse.
- APHIS-permitted (especially Select Agent) material movement is restricted; permits ask for all routes of travel to be documented.

## Termination: sterilization, disinfection, disposal
Devitalize experimental material before it leaves the greenhouse/lab or goes to landfill. Termination and its validation belong in the experimental plan. The PI is responsible. An IBC policy can define acceptable methods by level and volume, and should cover abandoned material (PI death, resignation, oversight).

Methods: heat (steam, hot water, incineration, heating coils), chemicals, freezing, composting, desiccation.

**Heat figures from the guide:**
- Heating to **85–100 °C for 30 minutes** kills almost all plant-associated organisms.
- Soil pasteurization with "cool" aerated steam at **70 °C for 30 minutes** spares beneficial soil organisms.
- Electric-coil sterilization boxes deliver **60–93 °C**.
- APHIS guidance for fungal, viral or nematode pathogens under permit: soil and solid waste at **≥104 °C for 3 hours** before disposal.
- Autoclave plants, parts, containers and media at **15–30 psi and 121 °C for 15–180 minutes**, depending on material type and state.
- Validate: parts of a load often fail to reach temperature. Use time/temperature criteria for the target organism, autoclave test strips, and maintained, tested equipment.
- At higher containment, autoclave everything leaving the greenhouse; a double-door pass-through autoclave is recommended. Liquid effluent: batch or pass-through sterilizer before the sewer; usually cool before release. Site expensive sterilizers to serve several zones.

**Chemicals:**
- Methyl bromide, chloropicrin etc. are being replaced by steam over toxicity concerns.
- Ethylene oxide (EO) chambers for equipment that autoclave heat (>60 °C) would damage; vaporized hydrogen peroxide (VHP) generators sterilize exposed surfaces with no toxic residue, ideal for small labs and workrooms. Both need special equipment.
- Common lab disinfectants: sodium hypochlorite, phenols, formaldehyde, glutaraldehyde, alcohol. Grower-supply chlorine and non-chlorine greenhouse disinfectants are available.
- BL2-P gravel under benches: e.g., 10% sodium hypochlorite (household bleach) solution.
- Clean all growing surfaces periodically (cleaning solution or soap and water); cleaning also prepares surfaces for VHP or other sterilization. BMBL Appendix B covers disinfectants in depth.

**Other:**
- Freezing kills adult arthropods; limited as a sterilant.
- Composting suits large volumes of material that poses no recognized environmental harm.
- Seedless plants: desiccate by withholding water, or chop/mince into pieces that can't grow.
- Incineration for dry combustible material, but some seeds (e.g., cottonseed) don't burn easily and local ordinances may forbid it.
- Tiny transgenic seed: secure fine-mesh bags over flower heads before disposal; work on dampened white paper (e.g., BenchKote™) to recover scattered seed.
- A quick spray, vapor or wipe-down is not adequate on its own; match the method to the organism.

## Pest control
Required by NIH, APHIS and others. Rodents and birds carry seed out; insects move pollen and pathogens.
- Screens recommended at BL1-P, required at BL2-P to exclude pollinators and birds; BL2-P exhaust fan louvers open only when fans run.
- Seal perimeters at every level against rodents and larger pests.
- Fumigation or sprays for pests like whiteflies; biological control (predators, parasites, parasitoids).
- Hot water and detergent with a power washer between runs.
- "Bake out" rooms at **40–45 °C for 2–3 days**; don't overheat equipment.
- When pests are part of the experiment, use selective controls. After vectors transmit a GM virus, eliminate the vector.
- Monitor the program's effectiveness.
- Use handling protocols to stop pathogen spread inside the facility (e.g., TMV; see templates.md).

## Training and reference manual
- Prepare a manual covering all safety and permit points; staff read, understand and agree to it before entering.
- Train interactively with the PI, greenhouse manager and safety staff.
- BL2-P and up: manual must include emergency and contingency plans and routine-operation documents. Experimental protocols optional but helpful; manual sections can go into the IBC submission.
- Brief all staff (even part-time waterers and tradespeople) on the research, organisms and special procedures, e.g., if transgenic root microbes are tested, runoff may need collecting and treating.

## Monitoring containment
- Detect escapes with susceptible sentinel plants, insect traps and spore/pollen catchers inside and outside.
- Example: in a caged insect-vectored disease system, monitor uninfected plants outside the cage in the same room.
- Night-time corridor light traps reveal insects escaping rooms.
- High containment: test the envelope for leaks, and check HEPA filtration, biosafety cabinets and sterilizers annually.

## Loss of containment
- Causes: equipment failure, fire, flood, storm, and especially human error (open doors, unlabeled transgenic material in ordinary trash).
- BL1-P loss: minor consequences, no response required. BL2-P and up: bigger concern.
- Above BL1-P: alarm system for mechanical or weather events; environmental controls with integrated local and remote alarms; designated people alerted promptly.
- BL2-P and up: APHIS and NIH required written contingency plans (including theft and vandalism), drawn up by BSO and/or IBC with the PI: measures to contain the breach, notification sequence, decontamination procedures, and contacts for repair people, researchers, authorities and staff.
- Reporting (2008): PI reports inadvertent BL2-P+ releases immediately, in writing, to the BSO (if any), greenhouse manager, IBC, NIH Office of Biotechnology Activities and/or other designated authorities. Escaped or stolen APHIS-regulated material: report verbally and in writing **within 24 hours** to APHIS-BRS Compliance and Inspection. The guide's phone number and email date from 2008; get current contacts from APHIS or the permit.

## Records
- All levels: records of experiments in progress.
- BL2-P and up: plus all plants and plant-associated organisms entering or leaving (barcodes help).
- BL4-P: dates and times of personnel visits.
- PI is the logical record keeper; someone in the facility (e.g., greenhouse manager) keeps entry/exit logs.
- APHIS material, Select Agents and PMPs: follow permit instructions. Select Agents need documented biosafety, security and incident-response plans plus transfer, training, equipment, inventory and access records.
- Keep a central repository in or near the facility.

## Inspections
- Inspect on a regular schedule and whenever new kinds of material arrive. Inspectors: greenhouse manager, BSO, IBC representative, state agriculture officials.
- Checklists support IBC approval, internal monitoring and compliance documentation; tailor one per containment level when rooms differ. See templates.md for the APHIS worksheet and minimum questions.
- Re-inspect periodically: light, heat and water degrade equipment and structure, and adherence to special procedures relaxes over time.
- An APHIS-PPQ 526 permit (moving live plant pests, biocontrol agents, noxious weeds) required a facility inspection. USDA generally doesn't certify greenhouses unless you hold or seek a permit. Select Agent facilities are inspected by APHIS or CDC.
- PPQ used a 22-page "Inventory of Containment Facility for All Plant Pathogens" (rev. 11/01), completed only by a PPQ officer, to decide if a facility can contain a given organism.
- After inspection APHIS issues an adequacy letter; unannounced re-visits during business hours were a Standard Permit Condition.

## Security
- Vandalism is a real risk; opponents of rDNA research have damaged greenhouse and field trials. Entry by force, defeating hardware, or being let in (propped doors, unlocked rooms, unchallenged strangers). All users share responsibility.
- For politically motivated threats, consider a response team: senior administrator, public information officer, facility manager, legal counsel, others; review deterrents and prepare fast, clear public responses.
- USDA authorized armed on-site security for BSL-3 and higher federal facilities.
- Select Agents: personal security risk assessment for anyone handling them; physical barriers or escorts for others; maintenance staff without clearance are escorted and have no direct access; at least three locking barriers between outside and the agent.

## Standard Operating Procedures
Required when working with organisms under APHIS permit. The containment officer (or similar) writes, updates and implements them. Typical SOP topics:
- Using, maintaining and disinfecting the facility and equipment
- Responding to emergencies
- Maintaining security
- Managing visitors
- Handling a containment breach
- Replacing glazing in a glasshouse

SOPs are living documents: revise with new permits, practices, equipment, people and technology; review at least annually. The APHIS-PPQ SOP outline is the guide's Appendix IV (not in the extracted text; see the PDF or current APHIS materials).
